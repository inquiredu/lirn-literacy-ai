# Content review: Literacy Across Modalities

November 15, 2026. A review of `deck.js` (28 slides, 6 parts, 60 minutes) against the November 12 planning meeting notes, for a one-hour LiRN session on Zoom that includes elementary educators. Citations were checked against publisher, arXiv and author pages on the web; what couldn't be checked is listed at the end.

**Overall: 3.2 of 5.** The design is sound and fits the meeting's intent. It needs three things before it's ready: corrected citations, a Zoom-native rebuild of the pauses, and widened representation, especially for elementary.

## Strengths

1. **The opening models the content.** The room reads the session's own Gemini-drafted description as text analysts, then sees how it was made. This is honest and memorable.
2. **The Four Resources model is used correctly.** The deck says "not a ladder," but the meeting's Gemini summary called the roles "upper tiers." The deck got right what the AI summary got wrong, and that contrast is itself usable material.
3. **Evidence is offered with humility.** Each research card names a signal, a gap and a question for practice, which matches Sean's "let's not hold any of our conclusions now as conclusions."
4. **The tension is real.** The Andrew–Sean dialogue on AI text adaptation (the grade-level text talking point against the newcomer it doesn't account for) is unscripted disagreement, and it is the most valuable three minutes of the hour.
5. **The v1→v2 bookend** gives a 60-minute session a visible arc and an outcome that participants produce themselves.
6. **Protections are named:** under-13 use, district approvals, teacher-generated output, made-up cases.

## Gaps: elementary

The session description promises "elementary and secondary educators." The meeting chose intermediate grades and set decoding aside as outside the presenters' expertise. That's a defensible boundary, but an elementary leader currently sees K–2 only as a parking-lot item. LiRN members work under the Minnesota READ Act (structured literacy, evidence-based materials), and AI is arriving in early-reading platforms right now.

**Plan:**
1. Turn Sean's signpost question into a real 3- to 4-minute beat at the start of Part 1. It stays inside the presenters' lane: no claims about teaching decoding, only the questions a leader asks of a K–2 AI platform. For example: Does it replace the teacher's ear for the child's reading? Does it keep decodable text decodable? Does practice end in a human conversation? What data leaves the building?
2. Add a K–2 case beside A and B. Case C: a first-grade teacher is offered an AI read-aloud tutor that listens and corrects. Use the same "preserve / test" pause.
3. Label every case and research card with its grade band, so the elementary and secondary evidence gaps are visible at a glance (see the evidence map below).
4. Add one elementary-native checking move. The Charlotte's Web activity already fits, so name the grade band on the slide.
5. Decide whether secondary gets equal weight. The description says yes; the meeting said intermediate. This one is for you two.

## Graphic opportunities

1. **Four roles as a quadrant or cycle, not a list** (`four-roles`). A numbered list visually implies the ladder the deck says it isn't. A 2×2 with arrows both ways fixes that.
2. **How this text was made as a swimlane** (`how-made`): Andrew and Sean, Gemini, Claude, the humans again. Each human decision point gets the ✦.
3. **Bastani as a small paired bar chart** (`noticing-a`): practice versus exam for no AI, GPT Base and GPT Tutor. Use only the paper's own figures, which still need to be read from the PDF (the +48% and +127% in circulation come from secondary sources).
4. **An evidence map** (new slide, or folded into `noticing-nav`): grade bands (K–2, 3–5, 6–8, 9–12, adult) against what was studied. The empty cells show the gaps in one look, and they make the elementary gap visible.
5. **An annotated AI answer** (`what-was-in-it`): the Charlotte's Web paragraph with each claim highlighted by the kind of checking it needs (memory, source, uncheckable).
6. **A true T-chart** (`t-chart`): two columns with a center rule, filled live from the chat on the next slide.
7. **Bridge or substitute as a three-question flow** (`bridge`): each question forks to "bridge" or "substitute."
8. **The hour as a bar** on the landing page and the plan slide, with presenter time and participant time in two tones. It proves "working session" at a glance.
9. **The real Gemini meeting summary as a check-this text.** It contradicts itself (16 registrants in one place, 50 in another) and calls the four roles "tiers." Real AI output from the session's own planning is a stronger artifact than an invented one, used with care and no personal details.
